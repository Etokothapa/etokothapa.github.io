Executive Thesis Summary: Investigated how multi-dimensional service attributes influence retail depositor turnover in Butwal's dense banking hub. Using SPSS regression diagnostics, the findings reveal that physical accessibility/distance (β = 0.605, p = 0.000) is the strongest determinant driving bank switching, closely followed by institutional reputation (β = 0.285, p = 0.000) and service delivery quality (β = 0.082, p = 0.039). In contrast, promotional advertising and marginal price adjustments were found to have no statistically significant impact on actual switching choices.

Push-Pull-Mooring (PPM) Model

Adapted from Bansal et al. (2005). Deconstructs bank turnover into Push factors (service friction, inconvenient branches), Pull factors (reputable competitors, superior accessibility), and Mooring elements (habitual inertia and switching friction).

Consumer Switching Model

Rooted in Keaveney's (1995) taxonomy classifying core service failures, service encounter failures (unhelpful staff), pricing/ethics concerns, and geographical inconvenience as core migration triggers.

Expectation Confirmation (ECT)

Synthesized from Oliver (1980). Evaluates how cognitive disconfirmation between anticipated digital/branch services and actual operational execution breeds customer departure in emerging financial markets.

Theory of Planned Behaviour

Based on Ajzen (1991). Maps how depositors' subjective norms, individual attitudes toward banking security, and perceived ease of switching govern behavioral migration decisions.

1. Optimize Physical Footprint & ATM Density

Because Distance was proved as the primary predictor (β = 0.605), banks must strategicially locate ATMs and smart digital kiosks across high-density commerce corridors rather than relying exclusively on online channels.

2. Safeguard Corporate Reputation & Trust

Reputation is the second-strongest anchor (β = 0.285). Transparent communication during service disruptions, zero hidden fees, and rapid dispute handling prevent reputation degradation.

3. Cut Queue Times & Enhance Service Velocity

Service quality impacts churn (β = 0.082). Banks should train frontline personnel in empathic query resolution and deploy automated queue distribution systems to eliminate customer wait times.

4. Align Marketing to Operational Reality

Since promotional advertising showed no statistically significant retention effect (p = 0.449), marketing budgets should pivot toward loyalty incentives, seamless mobile banking uptime, and merchant integrations.

Abstract

This research was conducted to investigate the important factors affecting customer switching behaviour among commercial banks. As the dependent variable, customer switching behaviour was considered, whereas reputation, price change, service quality, advertisement, and distance were independent variables. A structured questionnaire was used to gather data on 384 customers of the commercial banks in the Sub-Metropolitan City of Butwal.

SPSS software was utilized to calculate statistical measures including frequency, percentage, mean, standard deviation, correlation, and multiple regression analysis. The results indicate that distance (β = 0.605, p = 0.000) and reputation (β = 0.285, p = 0.000) play the most vital role in driving customer switching behaviour. Service quality was also significant (β = 0.082, p = 0.039), while price change and advertisement were not statistically significant in the regression analysis ($R^2 = 0.734, F = 208.55$).
